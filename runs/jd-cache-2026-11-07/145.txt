About the role: 
 Embedded Software Engineer – Working Student / Intern Position (Full-Time, 12 Months)
 Join our Drone Port team as Embedded Software Engineer and help develop the embedded software that enables safe and autonomous drone operations. As an experienced student, you will contribute practical engineering skills to a team of 20 colleagues and work closely with embedded software engineers, system architects, and hardware developers.
 Over this 12-month, full-time position, you will take ownership of defined development tasks, from implementation and debugging to testing and documentation, with support and technical guidance from experienced colleagues.
 What is your Day to Day Mission: 
 Develop, maintain, and optimize embedded firmware for our Drone Port systems.
 Translate technical requirements into software features and contribute to design decisions.
 Implement and test board support packages (BSPs), peripheral drivers, and interfaces to external sensors.
 Debug firmware and investigate hardware–software interactions using appropriate measurement and - debugging tools.
 Develop and maintain tests to verify functionality and improve software reliability and code coverage.
 Contribute to bootloader development and firmware update mechanisms.
 Support and improve our firmware build environment and GitLab CI/CD pipelines.
 Develop and maintain tools for production, device configuration, and testing.
 Document your work and participate in code reviews and technical discussions.
 What you bring to the team: 
 You are studying electrical engineering, computer engineering, computer science, or a related subject and are available to work full-time for 12 months.
 You have substantial practical experience in embedded software development.
 You write clean, structured, and efficient C code and understand the constraints of resource-limited microcontrollers.
 You have hands-on experience with ARM Cortex-M microcontrollers and common peripherals.
 You have practical experience with several embedded communication interfaces, such as UART, I²C, SPI, CAN, or Ethernet.
 You understand core real-time operating system concepts and have ideally used an RTOS
 You use Git confidently and are familiar with collaborative development workflows, including branches, merge requests, and code reviews.
 You can read electronic schematics and use them to investigate hardware–software issues.
 You approach debugging systematically and can explain your findings clearly.
 You work independently on agreed tasks, communicate progress and obstacles proactively, and seek input when needed.
 You enjoy collaborating across disciplines and sharing technical knowledge.
 Your English is at least B2 level, both written and spoken.
